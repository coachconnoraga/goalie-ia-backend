# THE FLGA METHODOLOGY: THE 6-LAYER FRAMEWORK

## LAYER 1: STANCE (Cue: "Hips back. Chest big. Knees bent. Stick up.")
- Feet width: Wider than shoulder width (shoulder width is the minimum).
- Feet angle: Turned out 15–45°, both equally.
- Knees: A few inches of bend up to a quarter squat. Never a full squat. Never locked out. 
- Weight: Balls of the feet, right behind the toes.
- Hips & Chest: Hips hinged back (not sitting). Chest big and tall. Back angled forward 10–45°.
- Stick angle: 10–45° of shaft angle. Head flat to the shooter.
- Hands: Elbows bent ~90°, still connected to the rib cage. Do NOT prescribe a specific distance off the body, just ensure 90-degree elbows.
- Stick height: The stick head must cover the gap between your shoulders and your head, sitting just above your shoulder line.

## LAYER 2: POSITIONING (Cue: "Five spots. Laser beams on the shooter.")
- The 5 Spots: Top center, top left, top right, pipe left, pipe right. 
- Depth: 1–3 inches above the goal line at every spot. Same stance at every spot.
- Squaring: Project laser beams from shoulders and hips. The SHOOTER stays between them.
- Moving: Short moves = choppy even steps, no bobbing. Long moves = drop step, hop, land set. 

## LAYER 3: FOOTWORK (Cue: "Wide lead step. Cut off the path of the shot.")
- Lead Step: Push off the back foot, drive the lead foot into the PATH of the shot. Step all the way to the pipe. NEVER step "at the shooter".
- Gather Step: Back foot comes underneath on arrival. Arrive balanced and set. 
- THE FIVE-HOLE EXCEPTION: NO lead step. TWO SEPARATE techniques:
  1. Squat Technique: Bring both feet together, get up on toes, squat straight down.
  2. Knee Drop Technique: Drop the preferred knee down to cover the gap.
  *NEVER push off the back foot for a five-hole shot.*

## LAYER 4: STILLNESS & EYES (Cue: "Still. See. Attack.")
- Still: Nothing moves until the ball is out of the stick. Still is loaded and quiet, not frozen.
- See: Find the stick head. Track the ball all the way into your own mesh. NEVER anticipate.
- Attack: Mentality. Go get the ball effectively by stepping into its path. 
- Flinch Cue: "See the ball. Attack the ball." Say it out loud.

## LAYER 5: SAVE TECHNIQUE & CLEARING (Cue: "Point A to point B.")
- Hand Path: The shortest route is a straight line. Hands go directly at the ball. BOTH hands (top and bottom) move to the ball on every save.
- ALL Low Shots: Get your stick all the way down. Catch if you have time, smother if you don’t. Stick head must make contact with the ball covering max surface area, flat to the shooter.
- Bouncers: REACT, do not anticipate. Step your body in front of it. 
- Body Saves on the Crease: If you don't have enough time for a low shot on the crease, keep your stick UP to protect high, and use your legs/lower body to block.
- CLEARING & PASSING TECHNIQUE: Always aim to throw the ball overhand. Point your butt end at the target. Get your hands up and back. The shaft should be flat (parallel to the ground). The top hand MUST be halfway or 3/4 of the way down the shaft (not just under the plastic—that is only for making saves). The top hand should be in line with your ear. The bottom hand must be right above or exactly on the butt end, using the stick as a lever. 

## CORE GOALIE FUNDAMENTALS: THE 7 SAVE LOCATIONS
- **Every Save Should Include:** Lead step wider than the shot path. Meet the ball with stick head flat to shooter. Half cradle to control the rebound AFTER the shot hits the mesh. Top hand moves in a straight line. BOTH hands move to the ball (never reach with just your top hand). Gather step to maintain balance. Hips square to the shooter.
1. Stick Side High: Both hands move straight to ball. Step, don't jump.
2. Off Stick High: Full step/extension. Get body behind ball. Turn stick slightly INWARDS after contact to control rebound.
3. Stick Side Hip: Drive hands out in front. Get body behind ball.
4. Off Stick Hip: Focus primarily on getting the lead step wider than the path of the shot. Keep your hips SQUARE to the shooter as you step towards the shot (do not turn away). Catch the ball in front of your body by extending BOTH arms at the ball. Keep the stick head flat to the shooter. Do NOT bend the bottom elbow too much and do NOT let the shaft go behind you; extend both arms out in front so the shaft doesn't get caught in the goal, maximizing surface area. Both hands move to the ball.
5. Stick Side Low: Bend knees 3-5 inches OR drop back knee. Move laterally.
6. Five Hole: NO LEAD STEP. Squat or knee down.
7. Off Stick Low: Like off-stick hip. Bend knees or drop back knee.

## LAYER 6: THE MENTAL GAME (Cue: "Stay on the seesaw.")
- The Seesaw: Balanced between Ego (leaning forward) and Doubt (leaning back). 
- The Reset (Fix it, Forget it, Focus): 1. Name what happened. 2. Tap Left, Right, Top pipe. 3. Say "Next play".
- Slumps: Stop taking empty reps. Find the specific rep you are bad at and drill it slowly.
- Burnout: Take days fully away from the sport.

# THE FIELD MANUAL & SITUATIONAL TROUBLESHOOTING

## Dodges from X and The "Drop Step Pivot"
- When a shooter dodges from X, get to the goal post when they are 1-2 yards away from Goal Line Extended (GLE). Hold your normal stance on the pipe, square to the shooter.
- If the shooter continues to push upfield (1 or 2 yards ABOVE GLE), do NOT pop off your post into the top right/left spot. Instead, use the **Drop Step Pivot**: Keep your inside foot planted on the goal post, and simply pivot your hips to remain square to the shooter. This takes away the easy inside shot while covering enough of the outside angle.
- Only take short, choppy steps OFF the pipe if they continue pushing 3+ yards above the drop step zone.

## Alley Dodges & Shots on the Run
- Diagnosis: The number one issue here is POSITIONING. You must frame your positioning to maintain a good angle to the shooter as they move down the alley. 
- Fix/Drills: Recommend "Walking the Arc" (have a shooter jog around the arc without taking a shot so you can practice maintaining correct positioning) and facing live Alley Dodges specifically targeting that stick-side low shot. 
- DO NOT suggest "Walk the line", "Stepping Progression", or "Pipe to Finish on the Run" for this issue.

## "I get my stick to the ball, but it bounces in"
- Diagnosis: The goalie is getting their lead step there, but they are NOT pushing off their back foot, or their lead step is not wide enough. Their body weight isn't behind the save.
- Fix/Drills: Recommend "Stepping Progression (cones + full steps)" (to fix the push and step width) and "Tennis Ball Chest Saves" (to train getting the body completely behind the ball). 
- DO NOT recommend "Knee low saves" for this issue.

## Crease Play & Exceptions for Coming Off Your Line
- Do NOT use "drop step" or "slide" language for crease shots (except for the Drop Step Pivot on X dodges mentioned above). Just stay 1-3 inches off the line and be prepared to adjust. Play your normal arc for top-center crease shots.
- Only step off your arc to steal angle for: 
  1. Low-angle shots where you are absolutely certain they are shooting.
  2. Question mark dodges from X.
  3. Time-and-room step downs up top with no pass option.
  4. Quick step-down shots off a pass at low angles.

## Short Goalies vs. Tall Goalies
- Short Goalies: Need pristine technique and rapid lateral footwork. Can play slightly higher arcs on the crease to appear bigger. Naturally better at low shots. MUST heavily emphasize training for HIGH shots. 
- Tall Goalies: Better to stay home and play passively deeper in the cage. Naturally better at high shots. MUST heavily emphasize training for LOW shots because their hands are further from the ground.

## Women's & Girls' Lacrosse Differences
- 100% of this goaltending methodology transfers to the women's game. 
- Shot Profile: Women shooters typically have quicker releases. You will see mostly overhand shots, but also sidearm and underhand shots from distance (less common, but they happen). 
- Clearing: Men have 20 seconds to clear over the midline. Women often have 1 minute or unlimited time depending on the rulebook. Women goalies MUST be much more patient; there is much less transition offense in the women's game. Look to slow the ball down.
- Equipment & Stringing: Women's and Men's goalie sticks are EXACTLY the same. Not shorter, no different pocket. The ONLY important equipment difference is that men wear protective cups, and women do not.

# OFFICIAL RULES & SPORT DIFFERENTIATION
################################################################################
## General Field Lacrosse Characteristics
- Both men's and women's games are played with 10 players on the field per team.
- The field dimensions are 91.4m to 100m in length and 50m to 60m in width.
- The game duration consists of four 15-minute quarters.
- If tied at the end of regulation, sudden victory overtime is played in 4-minute periods until a goal is scored.
- An 80-second shot clock is used to limit possession time.

## Men's Field Lacrosse Rules
- The 10 players are strictly distributed as 1 goalkeeper, 3 defenders, 3 midfielders, and 3 attackers.
- Body checking is permitted from the front or side, above the hip and below the neck. 
- A team can have a maximum of four long sticks on the field at any one time (excluding the goalkeeper).
- All players must wear protective helmets with facemasks, gloves, and mouth guards. Goalkeepers additionally require a throat guard, a chest protector, and a box/cup.
- Play restarts at the beginning of periods and after goals with a face-off at the center of the field.
- The Over and Back rule applies once the ball is advanced past the centerline and the shot clock is at or under 60 seconds. 

## Women's Field Lacrosse Rules
- Up to 10 players per team are permitted on the field, and one of them may be a goalkeeper.
- Illegal contact is heavily penalized; a player must not displace another player by holding or pushing with their body or crosse.
- Field players may wear close-fitting gloves, nose guards, and eye guards. Field players are not permitted to wear headgear or face masks. All players must wear an intra-oral mouthguard.
- Goalkeepers must wear a chest or body pad, a helmet with a facemask and an attached throat protector, and padded gloves.
- The Shooting Space rule prevents defenders from guarding the goal by obstructing the shooting lane with their body without legally marking an opponent.
- The Three Seconds rule dictates that a defender must not remain in the 11m Fan for more than three seconds unless they are marking an opponent within a crosse length.
- Play starts and restarts after goals with a Draw in the center-circle.

## WARM-UP / GENERAL / DIAGNOSTIC
- **Standard Lacrosse Goalie Warm-up**: DO NOT recommend dynamic body warm-ups or visualization when asked about pre-game warmups; DO NOT mention live shots unless specifically asked. You MUST provide this exact protocol for a standard Lacrosse Goalie Warm-up: 
  * 10 shots anywhere high, 10 shots anywhere mid, 10 shots anywhere low, and 10 shots anywhere on the cage. 
  * The first 30 shots should be at about 70% speed. The last 10 shots should be at about 80-90% speed.
  * Shots should come from about 10-12 yards out.
