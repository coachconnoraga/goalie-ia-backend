# THE FLGA METHODOLOGY: THE 6-LAYER FRAMEWORK
Diagnose before you prescribe. Find which of these 6 layers is failing and fix it. Fix lower layers before higher ones.

## LAYER 1: STANCE (Cue: "Hips back. Chest big. Knees bent. Stick up.")
The stance must hold for 5 minutes without breaking. 
- Feet width: Wider than shoulder width (shoulder width is the minimum).
- Feet angle: Turned out 15–45°, both equally. (Allows pushing straight off the inside of the back foot for lateral movement).
- Knees: A few inches of bend up to a quarter squat. Never a full squat. Never locked out. NEVER stand tall with feet together before the shot happens.
- Weight: Balls of the feet, right behind the toes.
- Hips & Chest: Hips hinged back (not sitting). Chest big and tall. Back angled forward 10–45°.
- Stick angle: 10–45° of shaft angle. Head flat to the shooter. This angle relaxes the top elbow.
- Hands: Elbows bent ~90°, still connected to the rib cage. Hands 6–8" off the body. 
- Stick height: Head above the shoulder line. 

## LAYER 2: POSITIONING (Cue: "Five spots. Laser beams on the shooter.")
Principle: Less is more. Stand in 5 places, and be still before the shot.
- The 5 Spots: Top center, top left, top right, pipe left, pipe right. 
- Depth: 1–3 inches above the goal line at every spot. Same stance at every spot.
- Squaring: Project laser beams from shoulders and hips. The SHOOTER stays between them. Hips and shoulders turn together. If you square to the ball in the stick, you pull your body off its center axis, twist your body, and expose the net.
- Moving: Short moves = choppy even steps, no bobbing. Long moves (pipe to pipe) = drop step, hop, land set. 
- Ball Behind (X): Face X. Mirror the shooter (if they go back right, position as if they are back left to sit in the passing lane). Stop mirroring 1-2 yards from Goal Line Extended and get to the post.

## LAYER 3: FOOTWORK (Cue: "Wide lead step. Cut off the path of the shot.")
Sequence: Lead step → Gather step.
- Lead Step: Push off the back foot, drive the lead foot into the PATH of the shot. Step all the way to the pipe. NEVER step "at the shooter".
- Gather Step: Back foot comes underneath on arrival. Arrive balanced and set. 
- The Stomp Mistake: Picking the foot up and putting it down 6 inches away. Covers nothing.
- THE FIVE-HOLE EXCEPTION: If the ball is shot straight at the body/five-hole, there is NO lead step. There are TWO distinct techniques, and goalies can use whichever fits their play style:
  1. The Squat Technique (for goalies who like to stay on their feet): Bring both feet together and get up on your toes as you squat straight down to close the gap.
  2. The Knee Drop Technique (for goalies who like to drop for low shots): Drop the preferred knee down to cover the gap.
  *NEVER push off the back foot for a five-hole shot.*
- Crease Exception: Inside 5 yards, if there is no time, abandon the back foot push and just get the lead foot in the way. 
- Recovery: If you go down for a save, get back on your feet immediately. 

## LAYER 4: STILLNESS & EYES (Cue: "Still. See. Attack.")
- Still: Nothing moves until the ball is out of the stick. No leaning, shifting, or dropping hands. If you move early, a good shooter sees it and shoots where you left. Still is loaded and quiet, not frozen and tense.
- See: Find the stick head. Track the ball all the way into your own mesh. Do not lose the ball halfway and save from memory. Do not read the shooter's body. NEVER anticipate.
- Attack: Mentality. Go get the ball effectively by stepping into its path. Still and attack are not in conflict: Still BEFORE release, Attack AFTER release.
- Flinch Cue: "See the ball. Attack the ball." Say it out loud to give the brain a job.
- Blind Wall Drill: Best drill for this. Shooter behind goalie throws ball off a wall. Goalie reacts only to the ball, removing all body-language reading.

## LAYER 5: SAVE TECHNIQUE & CLEARING (Cue: "Point A to point B.")
- Hand Path: The shortest route is a straight line. Hands go directly at the ball. Low saves go DOWN and OUT in one motion. Avoid the "loop" or "comma" path.
- ALL Low Shots: Get your stick all the way down. Try to catch the ball if you have time, or smother it if you don’t have time. Regardless of whether you catch or smother, your stick head should make contact with the ball covering as much surface area as possible, facing the shooter and where the shot came from. 
- Smothering/Clamping vs Catching context: Catching or getting the stick down sideways is the ideal because it's more efficient, saves time, and controls rebounds better. However, if time is short, smothering is the correct fallback.
- Bouncers: REACT, do not anticipate. If you can reach it before it bounces: step your body in front of it and attack it. If it bounces at the edge of the crease: wait, see the bounce, save it like a high shot. 
- Sidearm vs Overhand: Sidearm releases later/further forward. Do not time the release; wait until the ball is out. 
- Screens: If he can get it on goal, it travels through space you can see. Do not lean around the screen. Stay still and hunt the ball.
- Body Saves on the Crease: If you don't have enough time to get your stick to a hip or low shot on the crease, err on the side of keeping your stick up to protect high, and be ready to use your legs and the lower half of your body to block the shot.
- CLEARING (Cue: "Exaggerated lead step. Hands up and back."): Take a longer lead step than a normal pass. Distance comes from the step, not the arm. Hands up and back before the throw. Target a high corner out in front of the teammate.

## LAYER 6: THE MENTAL GAME (Cue: "Stay on the seesaw.")
- The Seesaw: Lean too far forward = Ego (freelancing, leaving the arc; the floor is Lava). Lean too far back = Doubt (quiet, head down, hoping the ball goes elsewhere; the floor is Mud). Goal is to stay balanced. Fast way out of the mud is to TALK.
- The Reset (Fix it, Forget it, Focus): 1. Fix It: Name what happened internally for 1 second. 2. Forget It: Tap Left pipe, Right pipe, Top pipe. 3. Focus: Say "Next play" out loud and bring the defense in. Do this after EVERY goal and big save. Takes 4 seconds.
- Pre-Game Routine: Must survive a bad day. Doable anywhere. Part 1: music/hand-eye. Part 2: exact same warmup sequence. Do it before practices too so it becomes neutral.
- Communication (The 5 Calls): 1. Picks (direction early). 2. Slides (hot and two). 3. Ball location when behind. 4. Cutters (direction). 5. Check call (when ball feeds inside). Communication is info transfer, not motivation.
- Film Review: 1. Did I see the ball or guess? 2. Where was I/am I giving up a specific part of the cage? 3. Did I step into the ball? Watch for patterns (e.g., 4 goals to the same corner), not moments.

# THE FIELD MANUAL (Game Situations & Everything Else)

## Crease Play & Positioning
- Crease Positioning: Do NOT blindly advise goalies to "stay on the post" for all crease shots. Only stay on the post if the shooter is dodging from X or at a very low angle. If the crease shot is coming from top-center, the goalie must play their normal arc positioning.
- Crease Rolls: Stay on the goal post as long as you can. When they come upfield, drop step while keeping the back foot on the post to stay square. 

## Exceptions for Coming Off Your Line (The Floating Arc)
Goalies should generally stay home and hold their spot. The actual exceptions where it is right to take a step off your arc to steal angle are:
1. Low-angle shots where you are absolutely certain they are shooting.
2. Question mark dodges from X (when someone fakes driving upfield, changes direction, and shoots while spinning back towards X/GLE).
3. Time-and-room step downs up top when you know there is no pass option.
4. Quick step-down shots off a pass at low angles where the pass has already happened.
*You must be CERTAIN they are shooting, and you never step during the wind-up. Read the situation, step, then set and stay still.*

## Short Goalies vs. Tall Goalies
- Short Goalies: Focus heavily on pristine technique, positioning, and rapid lateral footwork. Short goalies might need to take more risks by playing higher arcs in certain situations on the crease to appear bigger and take up more cage. They are naturally better at low shots but worse at high shots. Do NOT tell them to "stay low"—they just need to be ready to use their body.
- Tall Goalies: Better to stay home, play passively, and sit deeper (closer to the goal line) because they naturally take up more cage. Tall goalies MUST heavily emphasize training for low shots, because their hands are much further from the ground and it is harder for them to collapse quickly.

## Fast Breaks
- Arc positioning doesn't change. 1-3 inches off line. Call the shape (Triangle for 4v3, Box for 5v4, "I" for 3v2). Name who is hot and who is two. Only come off the line if absolutely certain they are shooting. 

## Women's & Girls' Lacrosse
- 100% of this methodology transfers to the women's game. 
- Differences: More shots inside 6 yards, fewer outside. Clearing allows 10 seconds (vs 4 in men's), so teach a much more patient clear. 
- Free Position (8-meter): Know the shooter. If she's a step-down shooter, take a step off the arc. If she's a passer/dodger, stay on the arc. 

## Equipment & Stringing
- Pockets: 2 to 3.5 balls deep. Channel must be straight, not a bowl. VERY LITTLE to medium whip. 
- Shaft: Attack length (can cut to 27-28 inches) for hand speed. 
- Helmet: Look through the TOP bars, not the middle.

## Slumps vs Burnout
- Slump: Wanting to play more, overthinking, chasing the problem. Fix: Stop taking empty reps. Find the specific rep you are bad at and drill it slowly.
- Burnout: Not wanting to play, unusual complaining. Fix: Take days fully away from the sport.

## Being the Backup
- Between games: Train and sharpen your ax.
- During the game: Be the best teammate on earth. Do NOT battle for your spot during the game. 

## College Recruiting & Outreach
- The Highlight Tape: 3 to 4 minutes maximum. Put your absolute best 3-5 saves right at the beginning. Include CLEARS. Cut the clip right before the wind-up. No slow-motion.
- Emailing Coaches: Subject Line: Class Year | Position | Name | Club Team | GPA. Keep body under 5 sentences. Include highlight link and tournament schedule. Email before every major tournament.

## Core Goalie Fundamentals & Specific Save Locations
- **Lacrosse Goalie Basic Save Technique**: A visual point of reference for all 7 standard save locations. 
Every Save Should Include: Lead step wider than the shot path. Meet the ball with stick head flat to shooter. Half cradle to control the rebound AFTER the shot hits the mesh. Top hand moves in a straight line. Gather step to maintain balance. Hips square to the shooter.
  1. Stick Side High: Hands straight to ball. Step, don't jump.
  2. Off Stick High: Both hands move. Full step/extension. Get body behind ball. Turn stick slightly INWARDS after contact to control rebound.
  3. Stick Side Hip: Drive hands out in front. Get body behind ball.
  4. Off Stick Hip: Most technically difficult. Perfect step. Hips MUST remain square to shooter. Both hands drive out in front (do not let bottom hand go behind the body).
  5. Stick Side Low: Body lower. Either bend knees 3-5 inches OR drop back knee. Move laterally. Catch/sideways stick over trapping.
  6. Five Hole: NO LEAD STEP. Either squat (bring feet together and get on toes) or knee down.
  7. Off Stick Low: Like off-stick hip. Bend knees or drop back knee.
  ▶ Watch — Lacrosse Goalie Basic Save Technique: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cEjSYPqpaBc
