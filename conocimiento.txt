THE STANDARD — HOW YOU EVALUATE PERFORMANCE
Save percentage doesn't tell the whole story. The real metrics:
THE SAVE PROCESS:
• Pre-Shot: Goalie is set, still, AND positioned correctly. No guessing or pre-movement.
• The Move: Lead step wider than the path of the shot. Push off the back foot. Hips stay square to the shooter.
• The Finish: Balanced landing. Ready for a rebound.
THE UN-STATS (Coachable Wins):
• The Chase: Beat the ball to the end line on missed shots (winning possession).
• Communication: Can you hear them from the sideline? Calling ball location, slides, picks?
• Decision-Making: Leaving the crease safely (out the back) under pressure? Picking off passes at the right time vs. over-extending?
• The Outlet: Bullets or lobs at the right time? Smart pass over hero pass?
When diagnosing, walk these checkpoints in order. Most issues trace back to Pre-Shot, The Move, or The Finish.

DIAGNOSTIC APPROACH — ASK BEFORE PRESCRIBING
For technique questions, ask clarifying questions before prescribing. Generic advice helps no one.
Example for "I keep getting beat low":
• Where exactly is the shot ending up — five-hole, off-stick low, on-stick low?
• Where's the shooter? Top center, alley, low angle, behind?
• What are your hands doing — dropping, locking up, swinging in a semi-circle?
• What are your feet doing — is your lead step wider than the path of the shot? Hips staying square? Bending at knees or back?
• What does it feel like — guessing, late, or just not seeing it?
For mindset questions, ask:
• Before games (anticipation), during (in-game spiral), or after (rumination)?
• More "choke" (tightening up) or "tilt/rage" (frustration after mistakes)?
• What does your pre-game routine currently look like?
Only after enough info, prescribe.

### COMMUNICATION SYSTEM — structure every call as NAME + COMMAND + VOLUME (loud/clear, calm/confident).
Defensive terms: "(Name), you're hot!" = the 1 slide. "(Name), you're 2!" = the 2 slide. "Fire!" = slide now. "Reco!" = recover after sliding. "Wheel!" = rotate.
Directing man on ball: "(Name), alley!" force down the alley. "(Name), upfield!" force upfield. "(Name), hips!" engage at the hip. "(Name), turn him!" force a spin away from goal (often at GLE).
Picks/doubles/cutters: "Pick (right/left)!" — and call it again if the pick becomes a slip. "Double!" double the carrier. "Cutter! (right/left/middle/behind)" — crucial for stopping easy goals.
Stick checks/ball location: "Check!" check the nearest stick on a dangerous pass near goal. Ball location is a SECONDARY priority (use at start of possessions, behind GLE, after out-of-bounds).

### MENTAL FRAMEWORKS (from The Fearless Lacrosse Goalie + Mental Checklist)
- Goldfish Mentality: move on instantly from a goal. Use a tangible reset cue (e.g., press a spot on the glove) the moment the ball hits the net.
- The Bucket System: Bad Bucket — acknowledge a negative thought ("you can't save this") then drop it as useless noise. Good Bucket — fuel reinforcing self-talk ("I've put in the work"; "that one went in but I made four great saves earlier") and accept it as truth.
- The Rule of Thirds: chasing anything hard, expect to feel good 1/3 of the time, okay 1/3, crappy 1/3. A bad day is just "the crappy third" and it's temporary. Stop blaming defense, bounces, weather, refs.
- BALL, BALL, BALL: a repeating focus mantra that drowns out negative self-talk during low-action stretches.
- Mental Checklist basics: sleep 8-9 hrs; water 3 L/day; eat clean & sufficient (protein after training, simple carbs before/during); strong confident body language (it's a decision, and it shifts your actual mindset); growth mindset toward failure; 100% effort in practice = effort in games; you are the leader of your defense — own the communication.
