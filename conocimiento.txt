## STRICT NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS (NEVER DO THESE)
- NEVER teach reading a shooter's body, hips, shoulders, or stick angle. Eyes go to the stick head to find the ball. Nothing moves until the ball is out.
- NEVER teach squaring to the ball. Goalies must square to the SHOOTER.
- NEVER anticipate bounce shots. You react. NEVER advise studying or scouting a league's shooters to predict bounce shots. 
- NEVER recommend angling the stick down or trapping the ball for low shots/bouncers. 
- NEVER advise moving "towards" the shooter for bounce shots. Goalies must step to get their BODY in front of the ball.
- NEVER instruct a goalie to push off the back foot for five-hole shots. 
- NEVER recommend standing tall with the feet together in the STANCE to "take up more cage" at low angles. 
- NEVER teach a high arc as a default. Low and mid arc (1-3 inches off the goal line) are the standard.
- NEVER trade a save for a catch. Catch when you can; block when you have to.

# THE FLGA METHODOLOGY: THE 6-LAYER FRAMEWORK
Diagnose before you prescribe. Find which of these 6 layers is failing and fix it.

## LAYER 1: STANCE (Cue: "Hips back. Chest big. Knees bent. Stick up.")
The stance must hold for 5 minutes without breaking. 
- Feet width: Wider than shoulder width (shoulder width is the minimum).
- Feet angle: Turned out 15–45°, both equally. (Allows pushing straight off the inside of the back foot for lateral movement).
- Knees: A few inches of bend up to a quarter squat. Never a full squat. Never locked out.
- Weight: Balls of the feet, right behind the toes.
- Hips & Chest: Hips hinged back (not sitting). Chest big and tall.
- Stick angle: 10–45° of shaft angle. Head flat to the shooter. This angle relaxes the top elbow.
- Hands: Elbows bent ~90°, still connected to the rib cage. Hands 6–8" off the body. 
- Stick height: Head above the shoulder line. 
- Mistakes in the last second: dropping the stick, knees caving in (bracing for impact), sinking into a squat (late on high shots), standing tall with feet together (gives up all athleticism).

## LAYER 2: POSITIONING (Cue: "Five spots. Laser beams on the shooter.")
Principle: Less is more. Stand in 5 places, and be still before the shot.
- The 5 Spots: Top center, top left, top right, pipe left, pipe right. 
- Depth: 1–3 inches above the goal line at every spot. Same stance at every spot.
- Squaring: Project laser beams from shoulders and hips. The SHOOTER stays between them. Hips and shoulders turn together. If you square to the ball in the stick, you pull your body off its center axis and expose the net.
- Moving: Short moves = choppy even steps, no bobbing. Long moves (pipe to pipe) = drop step, hop, land set. 
- Ball Behind (X): Face X. Mirror the shooter (if they go back right, position as if they are back left to sit in the passing lane). Stop mirroring 1-2 yards from Goal Line Extended and get to the post.
- Mistakes: Moving early and too far on dodges. Chasing the shooter instead of finding a spot. Running up to swat at crease shots.

## LAYER 3: FOOTWORK (Cue: "Wide lead step. Cut off the path of the shot.")
Sequence: Lead step → Gather step.
- Lead Step: Push off the back foot, drive the lead foot into the PATH of the shot. Step all the way to the pipe. NEVER step "at the shooter".
- Gather Step: Back foot comes underneath on arrival. Arrive balanced and set. 
- The Stomp Mistake: Picking the foot up and putting it down 6 inches away. Covers nothing. Feels like slow hands, but it's actually no footwork.
- THE FIVE-HOLE EXCEPTION: If the ball is shot straight at the body/five-hole, there is NO lead step. Bring both feet together and get up on your toes as you squat straight down to close the gap. NEVER push off the back foot for a five-hole shot.
- Crease Exception: Inside 5 yards, if there is no time, abandon the back foot push and just get the lead foot in the way. 
- Recovery: If you go down for a save, get back on your feet immediately. 

## LAYER 4: STILLNESS & EYES (Cue: "Still. See. Attack.")
- Still: Nothing moves until the ball is out of the stick. No leaning, shifting, or dropping hands. If you move early, a good shooter sees it and shoots where you left. Still is loaded and quiet, not frozen and tense.
- See: Find the stick head. Track the ball all the way into your own mesh. Do not lose the ball halfway and save from memory. Do not read the shooter's body.
- Attack: Mentality. Go get the ball. Still and attack are not in conflict: Still BEFORE release, Attack AFTER release.
- Flinch Cue: "See the ball. Attack the ball." Say it out loud to give the brain a job.
- Blind Wall Drill: Best drill for this. Shooter behind goalie throws ball off a wall. Goalie reacts only to the ball, removing all body language.

## LAYER 5: SAVE TECHNIQUE & CLEARING (Cue: "Point A to point B.")
- Hand Path: The shortest route is a straight line. Hands go directly at the ball. Low saves go DOWN and OUT in one motion. Avoid the "loop" or "comma" path where hands swing out to the side then down.
- Bouncers: REACT, do not anticipate. If you can reach it before it bounces: step your body in front of it and attack it. If it bounces at the edge of the crease: wait, see the bounce, save it like a high shot. Never angle the stick down or trap.
- Sidearm vs Overhand: Sidearm releases later/further forward. Do not time the release; just wait until the ball is out. 
- Screens: If he can get it on goal, it travels through space you can see. Do not lean around the screen. Stay still and hunt the ball.
- Catch vs Block: Try to catch when you can. When in doubt, keep the ball out with your body. 
- CLEARING (Cue: "Exaggerated lead step. Hands up and back."): Take a longer lead step than a normal pass. Distance comes from the step, not the arm. Hands up and back before the throw. Target a high corner out in front of the teammate.

## LAYER 6: THE MENTAL GAME (Cue: "Stay on the seesaw.")
- The Seesaw: Lean too far forward = Ego (freelancing, leaving the arc, trying to look cool; the floor is Lava). Lean too far back = Doubt (quiet, head down, hoping the ball goes elsewhere; the floor is Mud). Goal is to stay balanced. Fast way out of the mud is to TALK.
- The Reset (Fix it, Forget it, Focus): 1. Fix It: Name what happened internally for 1 second. 2. Forget It: Tap Left pipe, Right pipe, Top pipe. 3. Focus: Say "Next play" out loud and bring the defense in. Do this after EVERY goal and big save. Takes 4 seconds.
- Pre-Game Routine: Must survive a bad day. Doable anywhere (bus, car). Part 1: music/hand-eye. Part 2: exact same warmup sequence. Do it before practices too so it becomes neutral.
- Communication (The 5 Calls): 1. Picks (call direction early). 2. Slides (name hot and two). 3. Ball location when behind. 4. Cutters (call direction). 5. Check call (when ball feeds inside). Communication is info transfer, not motivation.
- Film Review: 1. Did I see the ball or guess? 2. Where was I/am I giving up a specific part of the cage? 3. Did I step into the ball? Watch for patterns (e.g., 4 goals to the same corner), not moments.

# THE FIELD MANUAL (Game Situations & Everything Else)

## Fast Breaks & Crease Rolls
- Fast Breaks: Arc positioning doesn't change. 1-3 inches off line. Call the shape (Triangle for 4v3, Box for 5v4, "I" for 3v2). Name who is hot and who is two. Only come off the line if absolutely certain they are shooting. 
- Crease Rolls: Stay on the goal post as long as you can. When they come upfield, drop step while keeping the back foot on the post to stay square. Only come off the post an inch or two when they keep pushing high.
- Coming off the line exceptions (Floating Arc): Low angle shots, question mark dodges from X, time-and-room step downs, quick shots off passes. You must be CERTAIN they are shooting.

## Women's & Girls' Lacrosse
- 100% of this methodology transfers to the women's game. 
- Differences: More shots inside 6 yards, fewer outside. Clearing allows 10 seconds (vs 4 in men's), so teach a much more patient clear. 
- Free Position (8-meter): Know the shooter. If she's a step-down shooter, take a step off the arc. If she's a passer/dodger, stay on the arc. 

## Equipment & Stringing
- Pockets: 2 to 3.5 balls deep. Channel must be straight, not a bowl. VERY LITTLE to medium whip (too much whip kills clearing accuracy). 
- Shaft: Attack length (can cut to 27-28 inches) for hand speed. 
- Helmet: Look through the TOP bars, not the middle.

## Slumps vs Burnout
- Slump: Wanting to play more, overthinking, chasing the problem. Fix: Stop taking empty reps. Find the specific rep you are bad at and drill it slowly.
- Burnout: Not wanting to play, unusual complaining. Fix: Take days fully away from the sport.

## Being the Backup
- Between games: Train and sharpen your ax.
- During the game: Be the best teammate on earth. Do NOT battle for your spot during the game. 
- Halftime: Get a full warmup so you are ready to go in cold. 

## Rules Young Goalies Get Wrong
- Men have 4 seconds to clear the crease.
- Running out of the crease with the ball and stepping back in is a turnover.
- Goalie interference only applies if they touch your stick BEFORE you release the ball.
